My journey into the world of reading

Hello, Rafael here!

Today I wanna discuss something that I think many people underestimate these days... annnnd it's reading.

I see everyone on youtube telling you some magic techniques that will help you become obsessed with reading, but the truth for me is that there is no secret ingredient. And I want to show it through my own journey in the world of reading.

From a young age I was always told that reading is good. It helps you better organize your ideas, gives you new perspectives on things you always considered to be in a one specific way, and I can go on listing all the benefits of reading for hours. But nothing worked. There was literally nothing that could pique my interest and willingness to continue reading. It had nothing to do with my curiosity. I was interested in many subjects at school, always trying to learn more, but I did it through online lectures and after-school discussions with my teachers.

Suddenly, everything changed on a sunny July day last summer. A friend of mine recommended James Clavell's Shogun. It's a book about 17th century Japan, where the emperor died, while his son was only 9 years old, so he assigned the ruling to a council of 5 who then started fighting to claim the power and control over the emperor's son. And as I absolutely love Japan and everything related to samurais, I couldn't resist and bought the book, though knowing that it'll eventually find its place somewhere on my desk after I read the first 20 pages.

Ironically, it did after I read two thirds of the book, which is roughly 800 pages. And this was when something started to shift. I read 800 pages of a book. I enjoyed it, and it piqued my attention. "That's a bingo" Hans Landa would say. But after I read two thirds of the Shogun, something strange happened. I just couldn't read anymore. I went back to the state, where I would barely read 20 pages and call it a day. So I decided to wait for the moment when I'll be interested in reading again.

It took 4 months... February 2026. To be precise, February 13th 2026 was the day I returned to reading. I read Orwell's "1984" in just a week; then Bradbury's "451 degrees Fahrenheit" in 4 days; and then Kafka's "Trial" also in a week. In just 3 weeks I read 3 books, which is insane. Then I started Harry Potter and the Methods of Rationality by Eliezer Yudkowsky, which is, by the way, amazing.

The initial idea of this post was to share my experience with y'all and the exact way I encountered this myself. For the past few months reading has taken a significant part in my life, and what's more important is that I enjoy it. I enjoy reading every single word, elaborating, and reflecting on what I've read. The thing is that there is no specific time for you to start reading. It will come naturally. I believe that there is no reason to read if you don't enjoy it. So be patient, try to find something that intrigues you and captures your attention.

This is the way!
